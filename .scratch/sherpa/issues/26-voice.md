# 26: Talk to it (English, on this computer)

**What to build:** speak instead of type, like talking to a voice assistant, and have the answers read out. The chat is for the occasional typing and opens with a phrase.

**Blocked by:** 22, 25.

**Status:** built; tested with synthetic speech through the real recogniser, in real Electron (microphone opens, speech works); not yet tried by the owner with their own voice.

- [x] Recognition runs on this computer: whisper.cpp's server with the English base model kept loaded (the app downloads it itself on its first start (voice/installer.ts; `npm run setup:voice` does the same) into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sherpa\whisper`; not under the project folder because whisper.cpp cannot open files below a path with accented letters). Measured here: about 0.5 s per spoken request with the model loaded (0.9 s with the full audio window; the larger small.en model takes 3 to 4 s and was not used). No audio leaves the machine and none is written to disk.
- [x] A voice window (always on top, hidden from captures) with an orb: green listening, brighter while it hears you, amber thinking, blue speaking, grey muted; it shows what it heard and why it did not react. It holds the microphone (echo cancellation and noise suppression on, no gain control), cuts the stream into utterances with a loudness based segmenter that learns the room (a request may have pauses up to 1 s in it; a click, a hum or 15 s of unbroken speech is dropped), and speaks with Piper (a local neural voice kept running, one sentence at a time so the first is playing while the next is made; about 0.12 s per sentence once loaded; 1.2 times faster, SPEECH_RATE; voice PIPER_VOICE, default en_US-lessac-medium) and, when Piper is not installed or fails, with the Windows voices through the browser speech engine.
- [x] Who an utterance is for: the microphone also hears a lecture on the screen, so it reacts only when addressed ("Hey tutor, ..." in any of the ways it is heard, such as "Tudor"), or within 12 seconds of the tutor speaking or being spoken to, or, while a task is followed, for the short things said during a task ("done", "what is next", "I don't see it", "stop"). Ctrl+Shift+Space means the next thing said is for the tutor.
- [x] Phrases: "open chat", "show chat", "let me type" open the typing chat (no wake word needed); "close chat"; "stop" or "end the task"; "new chat"; "pause" and "resume"; "next step" and "go back"; "clear the marks"; "say that again"; "be quiet" and "talk to me"; "mute". A question that merely contains one of these words is still a question.
- [x] Voice first: with the recogniser installed the chat is not shown at start; ask by voice, the answer is drawn and read aloud step by step (formulas as words: "5 squared plus 12 squared equals 13 squared"), the reading time replaces the fixed dwell, a new spoken request interrupts the old answer. The microphone is closed while the tutor talks (and 0.5 s after) so it does not hear itself. Without the recogniser the app behaves as before.
- [x] 21 tests (segmenter, WAV, intents, speakable text); the pipeline checked on Windows synthetic speech: 6 utterances found in a stream, recognised in 430 to 550 ms each, routed correctly (a request without the wake word outside a conversation was ignored; room noise gave nothing).
- [x] Microphone trouble (owner reported "no microphone"; here it opens fine and even heard the owner say "Hello, hey teacher, can you", which was then ignored because two greetings in a row and "teacher" for "tutor" were not accepted). Fixed: greetings in a row, wake word within edit distance 2 of "tutor" plus common mis-hearings, quieter threshold for laptop microphones (room measured at about 0.002). The microphone is opened with fallbacks (preferred settings, plain, then each device in turn), failures are explained in plain words (Windows privacy setting, no device, in use by another program), it retries every 8 seconds and when a device is plugged in or removed, clicking the orb retries, and without a microphone the tutor still speaks and the chat opens. 8 tests with a fake media devices object.
- [x] The wake word is configurable (WAKE_WORD in .env, comma separated) and defaults to Sherpa (the first default, "tutor", has a final R and a TY cluster that are hard to say and was heard as "teacher"). Mis-hearings are accepted ("Noah", "Novah", "no va" split in two), near misses of four and longer letter words are tolerated, three letter words must match exactly.
- [x] Conversation memory checked end to end (real app, real backend, real model: the second message recalled a code word from the first). The app now asks the backend `/health` for its `features` when it starts and says so in the chat (and aloud) if the backend is an older version that would silently ignore the conversation, or cannot be reached.
- [ ] Not done: a wake word detector that does not need the recogniser for every sound (a lecture playing near the microphone makes it transcribe all the time, which costs CPU); barge-in by voice while the tutor talks (use Ctrl+Shift+S or Ctrl+Shift+Space); nicer voices (the Windows ones are plain); other languages (English only by choice).
- [ ] Owner check: speak to it with a real microphone in the usual room and note what it mishears.
